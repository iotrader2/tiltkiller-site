Rules built by testing, not by guessing.

Every rule in our system earned its place. We tested it across three years of market data, changed one setting at a time, and kept only what held up in good markets and bad ones.

The short version

Most trading rules start as an opinion. Ours started as a question: which rules would actually have worked, across many market conditions, if you had followed them exactly?

To answer it, we rebuilt three years of the market, from November 2023 to November 2026, in 15-minute steps. We replayed every possible trade through a simulator, then changed one rule at a time and measured what happened. Each change was scored on more than profit: how deep the losing stretches got, how many months made money, and whether the result held up in every six-month period, not just one lucky stretch.

The rules you see today are the current rules. Every change is recorded with its full results, so nothing changes without evidence.

How we did it, step by step

Eight steps. One careful process.

1

Rebuild the market

We collected three years of daily and 15-minute price data for every stock and ETF on our list, plus earnings dates. For each trading day, we recreated the scan exactly as it would have looked at the previous close, so no test could peek at the future.

2

Start with a fixed entry

The entry never changed: a 15-minute candle that turns back up, bought at that candle’s high. Everything else — which stocks, where the stop goes, when to sell — was open to testing.

3

Change one thing at a time

Each test moved a single setting, such as the minimum trading volume, the stop size, or the market filter, and replayed every trade. Changing one thing at a time shows exactly what that setting is worth.

4

Build test grids

Where two settings could interact, we tested combinations side by side in a grid (a matrix).

5

Score on more than profit

Each test was ranked on total return in R, the deepest losing stretch (drawdown), the share of winning months, and account growth at several risk levels.

6

Demand consistency

We split the three years into six half-years. A rule that only helped in one of them was treated as luck and left out.

7

Check for mistakes

When a result looked too good, or the app and the test disagreed, we traced it to the cause and fixed it before going on.

8

Record every change

Each accepted change is saved with its full results and every simulated trade, so nothing ships without evidence.

What the testing taught us

The lessons that survived the data.

Let winners run

A few big moves paid for everything. Taking partial profits early, at a fixed target or a stretched price, cut total return by as much as 45% in our tests. Our rules hold the whole position and trail the stop as the trade works.

Trade with the market, not against it

Taking no new trades while the market is in a downtrend removed many losing stretches.

Strength beats a good story

The single best filter was simple: buy only stocks stronger than the market since the open. It cut the deepest losing stretch by more than 40%.

Fewer, better trades

Capping new trades at three a day made more money than taking every signal, because the late signals on busy days were the weakest.

More rules is not better

Many reasonable ideas — stricter strength filters, tighter trends, or more volume — made results worse. A rule had to prove itself to stay.

Expect quiet months

Only about 4 in 10 months made money in the backtest. The gains came in bursts, so patience and small position sizes matter.

The numbers, with honest context

Over the three-year backtest, the current rules took 398 trades and made +319.8R after trading costs. With 0.2% of the account risked per trade, a $100,000 account would have grown to about $185,000, a compound annual growth rate of about 22.6%. The deepest drop from a high point was about 5% of the account.

What these numbers do not tell you:

  • They are a simulation.No real money traded these exact rules over this period.
  • They are on the optimistic side.The rules were chosen by comparing many versions on the same three years, so the best one looks better than it will in the future.
  • A few big winners did much of the work.The two best trades made about a third of the total. Missing one big winner changes the result a lot.
  • The bad stretches can be deeper.When we shuffled the same trades into 2,000 random orders, 1 in 20 had a drawdown nearly twice the one history gave.
  • Stops aren’t guaranteed.If a stock gaps down overnight or on news, it can open below your stop and the loss can be bigger than your planned risk. Positions held overnight carry this risk.

Glossary

Plain meanings for the few terms we still use.

TermWhat it means
BacktestReplaying a set of rules over past market data to see how they would have done.
RThe amount you risk on one trade: the distance from your buy to your stop. A +3R trade made three times what it risked.
StopA price where the trade is sold automatically to limit the loss.
DrawdownHow far the account falls from its last high point before recovering.
CAGRCompound annual growth rate: the steady yearly growth that would turn the starting amount into the ending amount.
Risk per tradeThe share of the account you lose if a trade hits its stop, for example 0.2%.
15-minute candleOne bar on a chart showing a stock’s open, high, low and close over 15 minutes.
Grid (matrix)A table of test results where each cell is one combination of settings.
