The Rules Ragnar Lives By

Most traders don't lose because they can't find good stocks. They lose because they break their own rules at exactly the wrong moment: a stop that never got set, a position that was too big, a winner sold too early, a revenge trade, a trade entered right at the open before the first 1-minute bar even closes. These are the High Growth Leaders rules — a stratAlerts strategy. Ragnar’s job is to watch the market by those rules and send alerts for the way in and the way out — so you don’t have to stare at every tick or make a gut call.

What is tilt?

Tilt is when emotion takes over your trading. After a loss or a bad streak, you stop following your plan and start trading to make the pain go away. You take revenge trades to win it back, size up to "get even," and keep clicking buy long after you should have stopped. Tilt is how one bad trade turns into a blown account.

How the rules kill tilt

  • No revenge trades. After a loss, no jumping back in to win the money back.
  • Daily trade limit. Max 3 new trades a day. At 0.5% risk per trade, the most you put at risk on new trades in a day is 1.5%. Hit the cap and you sit out until tomorrow.
  • Losing streak shutdown. Several losing trades in a row and the rule is simple: stop for the day. Put the axe down.

Rule 1: Only trade leaders in the top sectors

Money flows into sectors, and the strongest stocks inside the strongest sectors tend to lead the moves. Every evening High Growth Leaders scores each sector, then scores the stocks inside the top ones. If a stock isn't a leader in a leading group, it doesn't make your watchlist. And no FOMO. When a stock is already surging higher and it's extended, you don't chase it. If you missed the entry, you missed it, and there's always another setup. Don't chase a longship that's already left the dock. The same goes for a stock gapping up huge at the open. If the gap runs straight into a ceiling of old selling — resistance — that's where sellers are waiting. Don't buy it.

A leader doesn't stay a leader forever. When the uptrend breaks, especially on a nasty high-volume day, it's off the list.

Rule 2: Position size from your risk tolerance, not your gut

How much you buy is decided by how much you're willing to lose if the stop hits, never by how confident you feel. Before your first trade, pick your account value and your risk per trade — typically in the 0.1% to 0.5% range. At 0.5% on a $100,000 account, 1R is $500: that’s what you’d lose if the stop hits. Size every trade from that number so a single bad idea can’t do serious damage.

Rule 3: The stop goes in right away

Not after you see how it acts. Not once it's "working." The moment you enter, the stop is set. A trade without a stop is a hope, not a plan. The High Growth Leaders alert sets the stop from the 15-minute candle before you click buy. No Viking sails without a shield.

Rule 4: A short morning window — done by 10:45 ET

Buy alerts hit Discord in the first hour after the opening bell. We check every 15-minute candle from the open through 10:45 ET, so you know your trade, entry, and stop early — then get on with your day. No all-day screen stare for entries. One clear morning window for buys. Sell alerts come later when it’s time to get out.

Rule 5: Protect and hold the winners

Cutting losers is half the job. The other half is letting winners work without giving the gains back. You get the buy alert with entry and stop, and a sell alert when it’s time to get out.

  • Trailing stop on winners. Winners are held with a trailing stop, and you get an alert when it’s hit — so you know when to exit without watching every tick.
  • Stop to breakeven once it's working. When a trade moves in your favor, the stop moves up to your entry. From there, the worst case is a scratch, not a loss.
  • No overnight hold without a cushion. Your cushion is measured in R, the amount you risked on the trade. If you risked 0.5% of your account, that 0.5% is 1R ($500 on a $100,000 account). A position only stays overnight if it's up at least 1R at the close, and ideally 2 to 3R. No cushion, no overnight risk.
    Why this matters: gap-downs. Your stop only works while the market is open. Overnight, bad news or a market-wide selloff can make a stock open far below your stop, and you get filled wherever it opens, not at your stop. A planned 1R loss can turn into 2R, 3R, or worse before you can do anything. The cushion is what lets you survive a gap like that without a real loss, which is why the rule is no overnight hold without one.
  • Red at the close is an automatic sell. No questions, no exceptions. If the position is red at the end of the day, it's out. You can always re-enter the next day if the setup is still there.
  • No holding through earnings. Any open position is closed before the company reports. No riding a name into the report.

Selling a leader the moment it's green is how traders end up with small wins and full-size losses. The rules are built to hold the good ones and cut the bad ones. Guard your plunder. Move the stop to breakeven and nobody takes it back.

Rule 6: Don't fight a bad market

Even the best setup fails in a bad market. When the overall market is weak, most stocks get obliterated with it, leaders included. So before any trade, the alert includes the state of the market. You'll know whether it's a day to trade or a day to sit out.

The sweet spot is when the overall market is clearly trending the way you're trading — rising highs, rising lows, strength that keeps going. That's when the odds are most on your side. When the market is tangled, flat, or rolling over, Ragnar sits on his hands — no alert. Cash is a position of power. And when the market turns choppy, with no clear trend and rallies that fail, stay out and don't overtrade. Go sharpen your axe, walk your wolf, or row the longship around the fjord. Even Vikings waited for good weather to sail.

Why alerts with rules — not just more tickers

More tickers are not the hard part. Following a plan is. Ragnar watches the market by the rules and sends alerts for the way in and the way out — buy with entry and stop, sell when it’s time — so you don’t have to watch every tick or invent a gut call mid-raid.
