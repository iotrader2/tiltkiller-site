The Rules Ragnar Lives By

Most traders don't lose because they can't find good stocks. They lose because they break their own rules at exactly the wrong moment: a stop that never got set, a position that was too big, a winner sold too early, a revenge trade, a trade entered right at the open before the first 1-minute bar even closes. TiltKiller is built around a set of rules, and Ragnar's whole job is making sure you follow them. Some of his enforcement is still being built, so a few of these are coming soon in the app.

What is tilt?

Tilt is when emotion takes over your trading. After a loss or a bad streak, you stop following your plan and start trading to make the pain go away. You take revenge trades to win it back, size up to "get even," and keep clicking buy long after you should have stopped. Tilt is how one bad trade turns into a blown account.

How Ragnar kills tilt

  • No revenge trades. After a loss, no jumping back in to win the money back.
  • Daily trade limit. There's a cap on trades per day, based on your risk per trade. Hit it and you're cut off until tomorrow.
  • Losing streak shutdown. Several losing trades in a row and Ragnar tells you to stop for the day.

Rule 1: Only trade leaders in the top sectors

Money flows into sectors, and the strongest stocks inside the strongest sectors tend to lead the moves. Every evening TiltKiller scores each sector, then scores the stocks inside the top ones. If a stock isn't a leader in a leading group, it doesn't make your watchlist. And no FOMO. When a stock is already surging higher and it's extended, you don't chase it. If you missed the entry, you missed it, and there's always another setup. Don't chase a longship that's already left the dock. The same goes for a stock gapping up huge at the open. If the gap runs straight into resistance, like an overhead moving average or an old unfilled gap, that's where sellers are waiting. Don't buy it.

A leader doesn't stay a leader forever. When it loses its key moving averages, like the 50-day, especially on a nasty high-volume candle, it's off the list.

No new trades within 3 days of earnings, either. Earnings are the biggest gap risk there is, and a great setup can be wiped out overnight by a single report. TiltKiller flags upcoming earnings so you can steer clear.

Rule 2: Position size from your risk tolerance, not your gut

How much you buy is decided by how much you're willing to lose if the stop hits, never by how confident you feel. Before your first trade, TiltKiller requires two numbers: your account value and your risk per trade (for example, 1% of your account, which is your 1R). TiltKiller does the math on every trade so a single bad idea can't do serious damage to your account.

There's also a hard cap: no position is ever more than 15% of your account. Even when the risk math says you could go bigger, Ragnar won't let one stock become your whole account. Never bet the whole village on one raid.

Rule 3: The stop goes in right away

Not after you see how it acts. Not once it's "working." The moment you enter, the stop is set. A trade without a stop is a hope, not a plan. TiltKiller calculates the stop from the 5-minute chart before you click buy. No Viking sails without a shield.

Rule 4: No trading in the opening chaos

The first 15 minutes after the open are the noisiest of the day. The opening range is where everyone panic sells, only to watch the stock recover minutes later. Trade it and you get shaken out at the worst price. The trading window opens at 9:45 ET and closes at 10:45 ET. One focused hour, on a 5-minute chart, on stocks you picked the night before with a clear head. Let the berserkers fight the first 15 minutes. You come in when the smoke clears.

Rule 5: Protect and hold the winners

Cutting losers is half the job. The other half is letting winners work without giving the gains back.

  • Stop to breakeven once it's working. When a trade moves in your favor, the stop moves up to your entry. From there, the worst case is a scratch, not a loss.
  • No overnight hold without a cushion. Your cushion is measured in R, the amount you risked on the trade. If you risked 1% of your account, that 1% is 1R. A position only stays overnight if it's up at least 1R at the close, and ideally 2 to 3R. No cushion, no overnight risk.
    Why this matters: gap-downs. Your stop only works while the market is open. Overnight, bad news, earnings, or a market-wide selloff can make a stock open far below your stop, and you get filled wherever it opens, not at your stop. A planned 1R loss can turn into 2R, 3R, or worse before you can do anything. The cushion is what lets you survive a gap like that without a real loss, which is why Ragnar won't let you hold overnight without one.
  • Red at the close is an automatic sell. No questions, no exceptions. If the position is red at the end of the day, it's out. You can always re-enter the next day if the setup is still there.

Selling a leader the moment it's green is how traders end up with small wins and full-size losses. Ragnar keeps you in the good ones and out of the bad ones. Guard your plunder. Move the stop to breakeven and nobody takes it back.

Rule 6: Don't fight a bad market

Even the best setup fails in a bad market. When the overall market is weak, most stocks get obliterated with it, leaders included. So before any trade, Ragnar tells you the state of the market. You'll know whether it's a day to trade or a day to sit out.

The sweet spot is when the moving averages on SPY and QQQ point the way you're trading: the 10-day moving average is above the 20-day, and both are trending up. The best traders call that a power trend, and that's when the odds are most on your side. When they're tangled, flat, or rolling over, Ragnar keeps you sitting on your hands. Cash is a position of power. And when the market turns choppy, with no clear trend and rallies that fail, stay out and don't overtrade. Go sharpen your axe, walk your wolf, or row the longship around the fjord. Even Vikings waited for good weather to sail.
