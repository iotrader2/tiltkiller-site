stratBacktest vs TradingView’s Strategy Tester

Two ways to backtest a trading strategy: TradingView’s chart-native Strategy Report, which uses Pine Script, and TiltKiller’s stratBacktest, a no-code backtester for stock strategies, now in beta.

This comparison is current as of November 2026. Features and plans change; the official TradingView and TiltKiller pages are the source of truth.

TradingView Strategy TesterA strategy report on the chart. You add a Pine Script strategy, or a built-in or community one, and TradingView shows how it would have done on that symbol.
stratBacktestA stock-strategy backtester. You build the rules in seven steps, with no code, and run them across a universe of US stocks and ETFs on three years of daily and 15-minute data.

These tools overlap, but they are not the same kind of product. TradingView is a full charting platform used for many markets. Its strategy tool — now labeled the Strategy Report, and still widely called the Strategy Tester — runs on the symbol on your chart. stratBacktest is narrower: it is for writing and testing a stock strategy, including how it would have behaved across many names, and how it would have sized an account.

What TradingView is good at

If you already work on TradingView charts, the Strategy Report is right there. Add a strategy, and you get an equity curve, total profit or loss, drawdown, the share of winning trades, a full list of trades, and (on higher plans) deeper performance analysis. You can compare the strategy with a buy-and-hold path on the same symbol. Executed orders are marked on the chart.

You do not always have to write code. TradingView ships built-in strategies and hosts a large library of community strategies you can add to a chart. When you do want your own rules, you write them in Pine Script, which is designed for this platform and can express almost any rule you can define.

Two other strengths matter if your testing needs more than a few years of daily bars. Deep Backtesting, on Premium and Ultimate plans, runs the strategy on all available history for the symbol, not only the bars loaded on the chart, with a cap of two million bars and one million trades. High detalization of historical bars, also on those plans, uses finer intra-bar data for fills. And because the report keeps updating as new bars arrive, you can forward-test the same rules in something closer to real time.

The usual limits of a backtest still apply, on TradingView or anywhere else. Past results do not guarantee future ones. TradingView itself warns that tests on non-standard chart types can use synthetic prices and look better than real trading would have.

What stratBacktest is good at

stratBacktest is built for a narrower job: a written stock strategy, tested the same way across many names. You set the universe, a market filter, setups, entry, stop, exits, and limits and size. Then you backtest on three years of daily and 15-minute data.

The differences that matter in practice:

  • No code. The rules live in a seven-step form, not a script.
  • A universe, not one symbol. You can test liquid US stocks and ETFs, or TiltKiller’s 662 theme stocks, with filters such as a minimum price and a minimum dollar volume.
  • Change one thing at a time. You can run several values of one setting, see total R and drawdown for each, and keep the winner.
  • Every trade on a chart. A searchable trade table, with the entry, exit and stop drawn on each chart. You can flag good and bad trades and get suggested rule changes, with the R each change would have made.
  • The account, not only the trades. An equity curve at your account size and risk per trade, including a cash-only (no margin) view. You can also test progressive exposure: a lowest risk, a highest risk, and a recent-trade window, so risk steps up as recent results improve and back down as they worsen. Risk examples in TiltKiller materials top out at 0.5% per trade. The idea is closely associated with trader Mark Minervini, and stratBacktest’s version is modeled on his approach. TiltKiller isn’t affiliated with him.

What it does not try to be: a charting platform, a multi-market terminal, or a replacement for TradingView. It has about three years of history, not a symbol’s full lifetime. It is in beta and invite-only. It does not forward-test on a live chart.

Who should use which

TradingView is the better fit if you

  • Already chart on TradingView and want the test on the same screen.
  • Can write or adapt Pine Script, or are happy to start from a built-in or community strategy.
  • Trade more than US stocks: crypto, forex, futures, or other symbols on TradingView.
  • Want the report to keep updating as new bars arrive, or need Deep Backtesting and finer intra-bar fills.
  • Care most about how a strategy behaved on one symbol over a long history.

stratBacktest is the better fit if you

  • Want to test a stock strategy without writing code.
  • Want the same rules run across many US stocks, not only the name on a chart.
  • Want to compare one setting at a time in R, and walk every trade on a marked-up chart.
  • Want to see the account impact, including progressive exposure, at a risk per trade you choose.

Plenty of people will use both: TradingView for charts and single-symbol work, stratBacktest for universe-wide stock-strategy tests. Neither tool can promise profits. A backtest is a simulation.

Common questions

Do I have to write Pine Script to backtest on TradingView?

No. TradingView lets you add built-in strategies and community strategies to a chart without writing code. To test your own custom rules, you write them in Pine Script, TradingView’s programming language.

Does stratBacktest replace TradingView?

No. TradingView is a full charting platform. stratBacktest is a stock-strategy backtester. Many traders will keep using TradingView for charts and use stratBacktest when they want to test a stock strategy across many names without writing code.

Can I test a whole list of stocks at once?

TradingView’s Strategy Report tests the strategy on the symbol on the chart, one strategy at a time. stratBacktest runs the same written rules across a universe: every liquid US stock and ETF that meets your filters, or TiltKiller’s 662 theme stocks.

Can I test progressive exposure in stratBacktest?

Yes. In Account Performance you can use a fixed risk per trade or a progressive one: a lowest risk, a highest risk, and how many recent trades it looks at. Risk examples in TiltKiller materials top out at 0.5% per trade. Progressive exposure is closely associated with trader Mark Minervini; TiltKiller is not affiliated with him.

Is stratBacktest free?

stratBacktest is in beta and invite-only. It is not offered as a free public tool. TradingView’s Basic plan includes basic strategy backtesting; deeper history and finer bar detail are on higher paid plans.
