stratBacktest

A stock strategy backtester: build and backtest your own trading strategies on three years of market data, then see what they would have done to an account.

Build a strategy in seven steps

  1. 1Universe
  2. 2Market filter
  3. 3Setups
  4. 4Entry
  5. 5Stop
  6. 6Exits
  7. 7Limits and size
  • Backtest it on three years of market data, daily bars and 15-minute bars, across every stock or TiltKiller’s 662 theme stocks.
  • Compare side by side on every step. Run several values of one setting at once, see total R and drawdown for each, and keep the winner with one click.
  • Read the results in R: total R, win rate, average trade, max drawdown, months up, and year by year.
  • Look at every trade on a marked-up chart. A sortable, searchable trade table with the entry, exit and stop drawn on each chart; flip through trades with the arrow keys.
  • Review trades. Flag the good ones and the bad ones, and get suggested rule changes that drop the bad trades and keep the good ones, with the R each change would have made.
  • See the impact on your account: an equity curve at your account size and risk per trade.
  • Test progressive exposure. Instead of one fixed risk per trade, pick a lowest and a highest risk and let it step up as recent winners come and back down after losers. What progressive exposure is →
  • Save every version with its metrics. Switch between versions to re-run them, compare them, and go back to what worked best.

What this looks like on a $100,000 account (simulated)

In the simulated High Growth Leaders backtest, built and tested in stratBacktest, tight stops and a market filter kept the account’s drops small next to the market’s. Here is what that looked like.

$204,113from $100,000 at 0.25% risk per trade, Nov 2023 to Nov 2026
26.8% a yearaverage yearly growth
−5.7%largest drop from a peak
−22.9%QQQ’s worst drop in the same window (price only)

In QQQ’s two sharpest drops in that window, the rules took one trade each. Every trade has a set stop from day one, winners are protected with a stop that trails up, and progressive exposure keeps risk small until recent trades are winning. Winners held overnight can stack, and stops aren’t guaranteed on gap-downs.
